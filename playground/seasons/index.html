Where we are in the orbit

The axis keeps pointing the same way in space. On one side of the orbit the north leans toward the sun, half a year later it leans away.

01

Tilt leans you toward the sun, then away

While your hemisphere leans sunward, the sun climbs higher and stays up longer. Half a year later it's the other way round.

02

High sun is strong sun

Light arriving steeply packs more energy into each square metre. Low winter sun spreads the same light thin.

03

Not the distance

Earth is closest to the sun in early January, in northern winter. Set the tilt to 0° and every day is 12 hours, everywhere.

04

Polar day, polar night

Past the polar circle (90° minus the tilt) the sun can stay up for 24 h or not rise at all. On Uranus that's most of the planet.

Sun's latitude = asin(sin tilt × sin orbit angle) (for Earth this matches tilt × sin(2π(day−80)/365) within a few tenths of a degree). Day length = 2·acos(−tan lat · tan dec), clamped to 0–24 h; for Earth the sunrise edge is set at −0.833° (atmospheric refraction plus the sun's disc) so numbers match sunrise tables. Other planets: their year is shown as a fraction, and day length as if their day lasted 24 h.
